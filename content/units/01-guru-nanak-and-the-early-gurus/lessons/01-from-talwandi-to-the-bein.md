Guru Nanak Dev Ji was born in 1469 in Rai Bhoi di Talwandi, a village west of Lahore. Today it is called Nankana Sahib, and it lies in Pakistan. His father, Mehta Kalu, kept the village accounts. His mother was Mata Tripta. His older sister, Bebe Nanaki, is remembered as one of the first people to recognise that her brother was unusual.

## A restless, questioning boy

Most of what we know about his childhood comes from the [[janamsakhi|janamsakhis]], collections of stories about his life written down after he died. They describe a thoughtful boy who argued with his teachers and was more interested in big questions than in the family business.

One of the best-known stories is the *Sacha Sauda*, the "true bargain". His father gave him money to trade at a market. On the way, he met a group of hungry holy men and spent all of it feeding them. When his father was angry, Nanak said he had made the most profitable deal possible.

## Sultanpur Lodhi

As a young man he married Mata Sulakhni, and they had two sons, Sri Chand and Lakhmi Das. He moved to Sultanpur Lodhi, where Bebe Nanaki lived with her husband, and took a job as a storekeeper for the local governor, Daulat Khan Lodi. People remembered him as honest and generous with the grain in his care.

Each morning, before dawn, he bathed in the nearby Bein river and sat in meditation.

## Three days in the river

Around 1499, the janamsakhis say, he went into the Bein and did not come back. His friends searched the banks for three days and feared he had drowned. When he returned, he was changed. He sat silently for a time, and when he spoke, his first words were remembered like this:

:::quote gurmukhi="ਨਾ ਕੋ ਹਿੰਦੂ ਨਾ ਮੁਸਲਮਾਨ" translit="Nā ko Hindū, nā Musalmān" english="There is no Hindu, there is no Muslim." attribution="As recorded in the janamsakhis"
:::

He did not mean that religions did not exist. He meant that God does not see people by the labels they give themselves, and that what matters is how a person lives.

## A way of living

From this point he began to teach. His message is summed up in the words that open Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, the Mool Mantar, which begins with [[ik-onkar|Ik Onkar]], "One Creator":

:::quote gurmukhi="ੴ ਸਤਿ ਨਾਮੁ ਕਰਤਾ ਪੁਰਖੁ ਨਿਰਭਉ ਨਿਰਵੈਰੁ ਅਕਾਲ ਮੂਰਤਿ ਅਜੂਨੀ ਸੈਭੰ ਗੁਰ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦਿ ॥" translit="Ik Onkār sat nām kartā purakh nirbhau nirvair akāl mūrat ajūnī saibhaṅ gur prasād" english="One Creator. Truth is the Name. Creative being, without fear, without hate, timeless in form, beyond birth, self-existent, known by the Guru's grace." attribution="Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, Ang 1 (one common translation)"
:::

He also taught a practical path built on three practices that Sikhs still describe today: [[naam-japna|naam japna]], remembering the Divine; [[kirat-karni|kirat karni]], earning an honest living; and [[vand-chhakna|vand chhakna]], sharing with others.

:::perspectives
Almost everything we know about Guru Nanak Dev Ji's early life comes from the janamsakhis, which were written down decades after his death and differ from one another in their details and dates. Some historians, such as W.H. McLeod, read them mainly as devotional literature and are cautious about treating individual stories as history. Others, such as Harbans Singh, see them as preserving real memories of the Guru, shaped by the people who passed them on. Both views agree on the outline: the birth at Talwandi, the years at Sultanpur Lodhi, and a turning point that launched his mission.
:::
