When his travels ended, Guru Nanak Dev Ji settled at Kartarpur, a town he had founded on the banks of the Ravi river. He lived there with his family for the rest of his life, and it became the first Sikh community.

## Living the teaching

At Kartarpur the Guru did not live off offerings. He worked his own fields. The three practices he had taught on his journeys became the shape of an ordinary day.

Mornings and evenings began with hymns sung together by the [[sangat|sangat]]. Food was cooked and shared in the [[langar|langar]], where everyone sat together in a [[pangat|pangat]], rich and poor, high caste and low, on the same floor. Visitors could see that equality was not just something the Guru preached. It was how the community ate dinner.

## Bhai Lehna

One of the people drawn to Kartarpur was Bhai Lehna, from the village of Khadur. He had been a devotee of the goddess Durga, but after hearing a hymn of Guru Nanak Dev Ji he came to meet him and stayed.

The janamsakhis tell many stories of his devotion and [[seva|seva]], doing whatever the Guru asked without hesitation, even when the Guru's own sons refused. Guru Nanak Dev Ji gave him a new name, Angad. It is often explained as coming from *ang*, a limb: someone who had become part of the Guru himself.

## A successor chosen by merit

Before he died in 1539, Guru Nanak Dev Ji named Angad as the next Guru, passing over his own sons. This decision set a pattern that mattered for the future: the Guruship would pass to the person most fit for it, not automatically to the next in the family.

A well-known tradition says that after his death, his Hindu and Muslim followers disagreed over whether to cremate or bury him. When they lifted the sheet covering his body, they found only flowers, which they divided between them. At Kartarpur today there is both a memorial and a grave.

## Guru Angad Dev Ji

Guru Angad Dev Ji led the Sikhs from Khadur from 1539 to 1552. His wife, Mata Khivi, ran the langar and became known for her generosity. Guru Angad Dev Ji is credited with developing and popularising the [[gurmukhi|Gurmukhi]] script, in which Punjabi and [[gurbani|Gurbani]] are written.

## Kartarpur today

Kartarpur lies in Pakistan, only a few kilometres from the border with India. For decades Sikh pilgrims from India could only look at the gurdwara through binoculars from the Indian side. In November 2019 the Kartarpur Corridor opened, allowing pilgrims to cross and visit Gurdwara Darbar Sahib without a visa.

:::perspectives
Historians agree that Guru Nanak Dev Ji spent his last years at Kartarpur and appointed Guru Angad Dev Ji as his successor in 1539. Dates for when he settled there vary between sources. On the Gurmukhi script, scholars generally agree that forms of it existed before Guru Angad Dev Ji, and they discuss his role as refining and spreading it rather than inventing it from nothing.
:::
