From Talwandi to the Bein

8 min read · 1469 – c. 1500

Guru Nanak Dev Ji was born in 1469 in Rai Bhoi di Talwandi, a village west of Lahore. Today it is called Nankana Sahib, and it lies in Pakistan. His father, Mehta Kalu, kept the village accounts. His mother was Mata Tripta, and his older sister, Bebe Nanaki, is remembered as one of the first to recognise his spiritual gifts.

As a young man he moved to Sultanpur Lodhi to live near Bebe Nanaki, and worked as a storekeeper for the local governor, Daulat Khan Lodi. Each morning, before dawn, he bathed in the nearby Bein river.

ਨਾ ਕੋ ਹਿੰਦੂ ਨਾ ਮੁਸਲਮਾਨ
Nā ko Hindū, nā Musalmān
“There is no Hindu, there is no Muslim.”
As recorded in the janamsakhis

Around 1499, the janamsakhis (traditional accounts of his life) say he disappeared into the river for three days. When he returned, he taught a path built on three practices: naam japna, remembering the Divine; , honest work; and vand chhakna, sharing with others.

Glossary
ਕਿਰਤ ਕਰਨੀ
kirat karnī
Earning an honest living through your own work. One of the three pillars of Sikh life.
How historians read this

Most of what we know about these years comes from the janamsakhis, written after the Guru's lifetime. Historians differ on how to read them. Some treat the details as history. Others read them as devotional stories that carry spiritual truths.
